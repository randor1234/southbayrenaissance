The mission

The center of Silicon Valley used to be right here.

Not that long ago, the gravity of this industry sat squarely in Palo Alto. Founders lived here, built here, and stayed. Over the last decade the locus of tech moved north, and the scene down here thinned out.

None of the fundamentals changed. The talent is still here. The weather is still insanely great. Our families are here. What went missing was the connective tissue: the dinners, the demo nights, the Saturday coffee where two people decide to build something. Most of us are too busy to get to San Francisco more than once or twice a month, so we are going to make the thing we want to attend.

South Bay Renaissance is a centralized structure for tech, startup and social activity across the South Bay and Peninsula. One shared calendar. One pool of venues, volunteers and sponsors. One community that anyone can host inside. Where there is clear demand and nobody running it, SBR goes and recruits the hosts to fill the gap.

Submit an event

From an idea to a full room, in four steps

A golf outing, an AI training deep dive, a kids playdate. The path is the same for all of them, and SBR does the parts that usually stop people.

01

Secure a venue

Claim a space from the shared venue sheet or bring your own. Every venue in the network has agreed to the same standard request process, so booking one takes a few lines.

02

Send us the details

Once the time and place are locked, submit it here: the format, the date, who it is for, and what you need from us.

03

We build the page and fill the room

The SBR team sets up the Luma with you and shares it with the sub-communities most likely to come. It also lands on the regional calendar.

04

We staff it and help fund it

Volunteers for check-in and setup, sponsors if you want them, photography from the shared pool. Afterwards, post the photos tagged #SouthBayRenaissance so the next host can see how it went.

No approval committee. If it is a real event that follows the values below, we will help you run it.

The commons

Five things every event needs. We keep a pool of all five.

Most good event ideas die on logistics. So the resources sit in one place where any host can draw on them.

Volunteers

People for check-in, setup, teardown and photography, so the host can actually host.

Spaces

Offices, garages, backyards and event rooms on a shared sheet with one request process.

Sponsorships

Companies that want to reach this region, matched to the events that fit them.

Shared calendar

One regional calendar so events stop colliding and nothing good stays hidden.

Tools and services

Photography, ticketing, AV and printing, so you are not sourcing all of it alone.

About us

Who we are and what we hold to

South Bay Renaissance was started in August 2026 by a group of founders, operators and parents across the Peninsula and the South Bay, and coordinated by the team at Tribe. There are hosts, volunteers, venues and sponsors, and a set of values every event that carries the name agrees to.

Anyone can start something

You do not need permission or a title. Pick a format, pick a date, and use the commons to get it in front of the right people.

Generous by default

People come to help each other build. Hard selling, aggressive recruiting and working the room for leads are not what this is.

Open to newcomers

Someone who moved here last month should be in a real conversation within five minutes of walking in. Hosts own that.

Honest about the deal

Charging for an event is fine, hosts making money is fine, free and sponsored are equally fine. Just say which one it is, and who the sponsor is, before people show up.

Family friendly where it says so

Many of us are here because of our kids. Events for families get planned and run with that in mind, down to the timing and the food.

Bad actors are removed

Harassment or repeated bad behavior gets someone removed from the ecosystem, and hosts hear about it.
